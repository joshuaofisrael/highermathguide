Finite sums

Arithmetic and geometric sequence calculator

Calculator · Runs offline in your browser · Educational only · Updated 18 Sep 2026

This tool computes the $n$th term and the finite sum $S_n$ for an arithmetic sequence (common difference $d$) or a geometric sequence (common ratio $r$). You enter the first term, the step $d$ or $r$, and how many terms you want.

The output is meaningful when the list really is arithmetic or geometric, and when $n$ is a finite count of terms. A finite sum always exists as a number (unless it overflows). An infinite series is a different object: it is a limit of partial sums, and that limit may fail to exist.

This is not a convergence test and not a computer algebra system for general $\sum a_n$. It will not run a ratio test, a root test, or a comparison test. For those, read the series explainers.

How to use the closed forms

Choose Arithmetic or Geometric. Enter the first term $a_1$. For arithmetic, enter $d$ so that each term adds $d$. For geometric, enter $r$ so that each term multiplies by $r$. Enter $n\ge 1$ and press Compute.

The default arithmetic run is $2,5,8,\ldots$ with $n=10$. You should see $a_n=29$ and $S_n=155$. Switch to geometric with the default $r=0.5$ when you want a shrinking product.

How to read the output

The large line reports $a_n$ and $S_n$. For arithmetic, $a_n=a_1+(n-1)d$ and $S_n=\frac{n}{2}(2a_1+(n-1)d)$. For geometric, $a_n=a_1 r^{n-1}$ and $S_n=a_1(1-r^n)/(1-r)$ when $r\ne 1$. If $r=1$, the geometric sum is just $n\,a_1$.

$S_n$ is a partial sum, not a verdict about $\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_k$. A geometric series with $|r|<1$ does have a famous infinite sum $a_1/(1-r)$, and this page will not print that infinite value. Compute it by hand only after you know the infinite sum exists.

Common pitfalls

Large $|r|^n$ overflows double precision. The page will say so. That overflow is not a convergence test. It is a computer running out of range.

A common mistake is to treat a large finite $S_n$ as proof that the infinite series diverges, or a small $S_n$ as proof that it converges. Finite pictures can mislead. Use a test.

$n$ is a term index and a count of terms here, starting at $a_1$. If your course indexes from $a_0$, shift the formulas before you compare.

Common questions

Does a finite sum decide infinite convergence?

No. Sn is the sum of n terms. An infinite series is a limit of those partial sums. You need a convergence test, or a known closed form that already assumes a condition such as |r| less than 1.

Where is the infinite geometric sum?

Not on this page. If |r| is less than 1, the infinite sum is a1 over (1 minus r). That claim needs the limit, not only Sn. Read the series explainers before you quote it.

What does overflow mean here?

The computed power or sum left the range of a JavaScript number. Try a smaller |r| or a smaller n. Overflow is not the same as divergence in analysis.

Does n start at zero or at one?

This page treats a1 as the first term and n as the term index starting at 1. If your notes start at a0, adjust before you compare answers.
