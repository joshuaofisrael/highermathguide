Dot, cross, length

Vector operations calculator

Calculator · Runs offline in your browser · Educational only · Updated 18 Sep 2026

This tool computes standard Euclidean operations on two vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ in $2$ or $3$ dimensions. You can ask for the dot product, a $3$D cross product, a magnitude, or a unit vector in the same direction.

The output is meaningful when the components are coordinates in the usual orthonormal basis, so length and angle have their school meanings. A dot product then equals $|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\cos\theta$. A cross product then points along a direction orthogonal to both vectors, with length $|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\sin\theta$.

This is not a computer algebra system for abstract vector spaces. It does not handle $n$ components, it does not compute a line integral, and it does not prove Stokes theorem. It is arithmetic on two arrows you typed.

How to use the calculator

Choose $2$D or $3$D. In $2$D the third boxes hide. Enter components for $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$. The default pair is $\langle 1,0,0\rangle$ and $\langle 0,1,0\rangle$, whose dot product is $0$ and whose cross product is $\langle 0,0,1\rangle$.

Pick an operation and press Compute. Dot product needs both vectors. Cross product needs $3$D. Magnitude and unit vector each use one vector, named in the menu.

How to read the output

A dot product is a single number. Zero means the vectors are orthogonal (or one of them is zero). A large positive value means they point in a similar direction. A negative value means they form an obtuse angle.

A cross product is a vector. It is orthogonal to both inputs when both are nonzero and not parallel. Its direction follows the right hand rule used in most multivariable courses. If you only needed an area of a parallelogram, read the magnitude of that cross product.

A unit vector has length $1$ and the same direction as the source vector. Use it when a later formula wants a direction and not a size.

Common pitfalls

Cross product is not defined as a $3$ vector in $2$D on this page. Switch the dimension menu to $3$D, even if the third components are zero.

The zero vector has no unit vector. The page will say so. Do not invent a direction for a vector with no length.

Components are just numbers. If your data came from a basis that is not orthonormal, the school formulas for angle and length do not apply without a Gram matrix. This tool does not know a custom inner product.

Common questions

What does a zero dot product mean?

The vectors are orthogonal, or at least one of them is the zero vector. For nonzero vectors, the angle between them is a right angle in the usual Euclidean geometry.

Why is cross product only in 3D here?

The usual cross product that returns another 3 vector is a 3D construction. In 2D people sometimes report a signed scalar instead. This page keeps the vector form and asks you to switch to 3D.

When does a unit vector fail?

When the source vector is zero. Division by length would be division by zero. A direction is not defined in that case.

Can I read an angle from the output?

From a dot product, yes in principle: cosine of theta is the dot product divided by the product of the lengths. Compute those three pieces, then take arccos yourself. The page does not print theta.
