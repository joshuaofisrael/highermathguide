Study pathways

Three self study maps through pages that already live on Higher Math Guide. Use one when you want an order, not a single article. They are educational routes, not official curricula, and not a substitute for a course or a tutor.

Choose a route

Audience, starting point, and time shape

The useful differences are who the route is for, which page you should open first, and what kind of work the sittings contain. None of these maps promises a number of hours. The shape is the point.

AP Calculus AB bridge

Audience. Readers who already differentiate and integrate in an AP Calculus AB course and want meaning, series, or a first differential equation.

Starting point. Calculus foundations if the rules feel rusty. Otherwise what a derivative actually measures.

Time shape. Reading plus short checks on the derivative, limit, and Riemann calculators. Series and separable equations add sittings when they are new.

Open this pathway

Linear algebra first course

Audience. Readers starting vectors and matrices who want geometry before a pile of formulas. Calculus can wait until the Jacobian.

Starting point. The linear algebra topic, then the glossary entries for vector and matrix.

Time shape. Picture checks on 2 by 2 examples. Proofs of abstract facts are slower and belong with intro to proofs.

Open this pathway

Intro to proofs

Audience. Readers whose calculus was mostly computation and whose next course will ask for arguments.

Starting point. Real analysis and proof habits, then epsilon delta intuition.

Time shape. Slower, because each checkpoint asks you to write and rewrite. A numerical table is scratch, not a proof.

Open this pathway

How to choose when two routes both fit

If you can compute derivatives but freeze when a definition says “for every epsilon there exists a delta,” start the AP Calculus AB bridge through the derivative and integral steps, then switch to intro to proofs for the epsilon delta explainer and the three written checkpoints.

If the next course is multivariable calculus, do the linear algebra first course through eigenvalues, then read Jacobian matrices in plain language before the multivariable and vector calculus intro. Matrices as pictures make that handoff shorter.

If algebra and graphs are the actual gap, do not start on a pathway. Read calculus foundations or the linear algebra topic first, and use the glossary when a word is doing real work. The site FAQ describes the same loop: topic, explainer, glossary, calculator, then a pathway.

Pages the routes share

These maps do not invent articles. They order pages that already exist, and a few pages serve more than one route.

  • Sequences, series, and when infinite sums make sense and convergence tests for series sit on both the calculus bridge and the proofs pathway. The sequence calculator adds finitely many terms. It does not test infinite convergence.
  • Epsilon delta intuition is optional on the calculus bridge and central on the proofs pathway. The numerical limit estimator is a picture beside it, not a proof.
  • Eigenvalues in plain language, the 2 by 2 eigenvalue calculator, the 2 by 2 matrix calculator, and the vector operations calculator belong to the linear algebra route.
  • Jacobian matrices are the handoff from linear algebra toward several variables. Implicit differentiation and separable differential equations stay on the calculus bridge, with differential equations first look.
  • Every route sends you to the glossary for the short form of a word, and to sources when you want an open textbook or course notes.

Common questions

Which pathway should I start with?

Choose by the work in front of you. AP Calculus AB skills that need meaning: the AP Calculus AB bridge. A first matrix course: the linear algebra first course. A first course that asks for arguments: intro to proofs. If you are unsure, pick the starting point you can actually do this week.

Can I follow more than one pathway?

Yes. They overlap on purpose. Finish the checkpoints on one route before you hop, so the order still does its job. A natural pair is the calculus bridge and then intro to proofs, or linear algebra and then the multivariable topic.

Do these pathways replace a syllabus?

No. A pathway is a reading order on this site. It does not list every theorem in a course, and it does not claim to match an exam board. For open textbooks and courseware, see the sources page.
